# Project journal — CEFR Spoken Response Scoring Lab

A Python evaluation lab that scores L2 English spoken responses from the Speak & Improve Corpus 2025 against human ratings, and evaluates the scorers themselves: correlation, weighted kappa, consistency, fairness slices, and ASR-error impact. The evaluation report is the product; scorer accuracy is a means. Built to close the speech-ML evaluation gap in my portfolio for language-assessment AI roles.

## 2026-08-31 — closing the design's own criteria: the deliverables no results review could find

Checked the project against `docs/design.md` rather than against the evaluation report's own §9, and found three success criteria open, two of them invisible from anything in `reports/`: **`error_analysis.md` did not exist** (one reference to the filename in the whole repo — the line asking for it), **intra-scorer consistency had never been measured** despite appearing in Premise 4, the Evaluation design and the success criteria, and **labelled feedback examples had never been built** (`grep -rn feedback` hits `design.md` and nothing else; no arm here emits prose). The pattern is the finding: §9's five recommendations are all generated from what the results showed, so a results-driven review structurally cannot surface work the report never knew it was missing — the design document is the only artefact that remembers the original promises, and nothing had been reading it for three sessions. The 28th's handoff file opened with "the tree is dirty, commit and push", which was already false when written down, and has been deleted rather than corrected. **The one analysis worth running came out of writing the report up:** §6 tests whether *aggregate* WER propagates into scoring error and finds it does not (+0.151 raw → −0.014 partial), which is not a test of whether a *kind* of error propagates — `n_words` is the forest's dominant feature, so deletions attack the feature the scorer leans on hardest while substitutions leave it untouched, and averaging them into one WER figure is exactly the operation that hides one inside the other. Split over a common denominator, **deletion rate keeps a partial correlation of −0.119 (p = .004) where WER keeps −0.014 (p = .73), and the sign is the opposite of the alarm** — once proficiency is held constant a transcript with words missing is *under*-scored, not over-scored, which is mechanically what a word-count-dominated feature set predicts. It **replicates on the LoRA arm** (−0.114, p = .005), a scorer with a different mechanism over the same 600 rows — an independent scorer, not independent data. Pushed no harder than it deserves: r ≈ −0.11 is ~1% of signed-error variance against a band-compression term of nearly a full band, five variables were tested so one false positive is expected at α = .05, and deletion survives Bonferroni while substitution does not; the operational conclusion is unchanged and only the phrasing weakens. Two case classes were informative in ways the design could not have anticipated — **hallucination** (top-decile insertions, mean WER 0.820, max insertion rate **6.15**, Whisper emitting seven times the spoken length) has *no* residual effect at all (+0.017, p = .68), so the most dramatic failure is the one that does not matter; and **total ASR failure is zero of 876**, the `n_words > 0` filter never fires, which is worth reporting because it is the failure mode a reader assumes. The licence forbids quoting transcripts, so the "five concrete cases" criterion is delivered as five **classes** cut at the sample's own deciles with counts, mean WER, mean human score and scorer behaviour — and the table carries an instruction, since every badly-transcribed class is also a low-proficiency class (3.07–3.62 against the clean class's 4.33) and must be read against its own mean score rather than against zero. **Consistency turned out to be a specified figure that cannot exist here:** `score_batch` takes one forward pass and reads eight logits at fixed token ids with no sampling, so pass-to-pass QWK is 1.0 by construction for every arm, trained and untrained — a number that would have satisfied the criterion, measured determinism, and read as a perfectly reliable scorer. The substitute costs nothing because both caches already existed: agreement across a benign **rewording** of the prompt, the 28th's two zero-shot passes (blank letters vs CEFR-anchored). Pass-to-pass QWK **0.512** (expected) / 0.244 (argmax) against 0.144 / 0.072 with humans — and since self-agreement is a *ceiling* on agreement with humans, the arm sitting a quarter of the way to its own ceiling closes off "the prompt was arbitrary" as an explanation of the floor, giving the 28th's conclusion a second independent line of evidence. The instability is real and large all the same (renaming the bands moves 60% of the expectation decode's assignments, 81% of the argmax decode's): it is validity that is missing, not reliability. No equivalent exists for the fine-tuned arm — that needs a second fine-tune under a reworded prompt, a training run rather than a decode, and is stated as absent. Feedback examples **retired** in a dated amendment rather than quietly dropped, and the wav2vec2/SHAP stretch goal declared out of scope on the reasoning that both restate results already in hand. One correction while reconciling figures: §6's 16.8% / 22.2% / 12.1% WER numbers are the **65-response** notebook-01 sample; over all 600 gold-covered responses they are 16.6% / 20.9% / 12.5%, and the disfluency erasure is now stated as **0 of 3,058** filled pauses rather than 0 of 333. Last, the pre-push licence check was promoted from a per-session habit to `scripts/licence_scan.py` (utterance ids, speaker ids and five-word transcript runs, matched against the corpus itself; `--history` walks every blob in the object graph), and **writing it down immediately found what four sessions of doing it by hand had missed** — a real utterance id and speaker id sitting in a comment in `src/speechlab/data.py` and in two cells of notebook 01, an illustrative example of the join key committed publicly since the June `src/` refactor. The blind spot is worth recording: every manual check scanned notebook *outputs*, reasoning that `utt` and `speaker` appear freely in cell source — which conflates a column *name* with an id *value*, and the first version of the script inherited the same error and found only the `.py` occurrence. Replaced with `SIXXXX-00000-P30000`, verified against the corpus as nobody's id; the two historical blobs remain in git history and purging them means rewriting published history, so that is flagged as the user's decision rather than taken. The scan also produced exactly the false positive its docstring predicts (notebook 01's own aside "*This is the most important comparison in the notebook*" colliding with learner speech), so it carries an `ADJUDICATED` allowlist of cleared phrases with reasons — and deliberately no equivalent for ids, which have no innocent explanation. Nothing changed a metric: 53 tests pass, notebook 05 re-executed with every pre-existing output identical checked cell by cell against `HEAD`, licence scan clean across all 42 tracked files. **Every design deliverable is closed and every roadmap item struck; what remains is research, not scope.** Full detail: `docs/session-2026-08-31.md`.

## 2026-08-28 — the baseline yesterday's arm never had: fine-tuning tripled QWK

Yesterday's write-up reported the LoRA transcript scorer at QWK 0.446 against RF's 0.558 and never established that the fine-tuning did anything — every number 0.446 was set against comes from a *different pipeline*, so "worse after fine-tuning" was comparing the arm to its neighbours rather than to itself. A grep confirmed no zero-shot run existed anywhere in the repo. **The untrained floor is QWK 0.144, so the LoRA is worth +0.302 — roughly a tripling — and the negative result is about the arm's ceiling, not a failed training run.** The uncomfortable part is how cheap it was: no training and no cross-validation (nothing is fitted, so all 876 rows are held out by construction), one forward pass, about four minutes against the hours the trained arm took, through a code path that already existed — `LoRABandScorer.__init__` has taken `adapter_path: str | None = None` since the day it was written, and reading the band distribution off the `A`–`H` logits rather than free-generating means the scheme survives an untrained model that would rather answer in prose. Implemented as `--zero-shot` on `scripts/finetune_lora.py` rather than a separate driver, deliberately: a standalone script would have had to copy the fold assignment out of the trained arm's cache, whereas the flag reuses the same `GroupKFold` loop on the same dataframe so rows and folds match *structurally*, with the notebook's parity assertions demoted from load-bearing wall to smoke alarm. A low zero-shot number had two explanations and only one is about the model, so a second variant went in: `build_prompt` labels bands `A`–`H` with no CEFR names attached — right for fine-tuning, which would otherwise fight pretrained baggage about "B2", but a handicap for an untrained model asked to use a scale nobody gave it the key to. `--rubric` names the anchors from data_gates gate 1 (1=A1 … 5=C1), leaving half-steps unnamed because CEFR has no name for the midpoint and inventing "B1+" would put a label in the prompt no rater ever used. Two guards shipped with it: the plain prompt verified **byte-identical** to the one the trained arm used (against a literal, not eyeballed), and `--rubric` without `--zero-shot` made a hard error, since `write_fold_data` builds training rows through `build_record` which does not take the flag — training with it would fit on plain prompts and score on anchored ones, and `assert_prompt_parity` cannot catch that because it checks the built string against itself. The result answers the confound: untrained with blank labels the model puts **797 of 876 responses in the top band** and the other 79 in the bottom one, a two-class answer with 90% of its mass on the two endpoints of an eight-band scale; anchoring fixes the collapse (five bands used, MAE 0.886 → 0.728, r 0.228 → 0.281) without fixing the ranking (QWK does not rise, it slips to 0.122) — the model relocates its guess from "everyone is H" to "everyone is G" and orders candidates no better for it, so the floor is the model's and not the label scheme's. Two measurement traps surfaced, both live in the existing write-up: **prediction range is worthless under class collapse** (blank-label argmax spans the full 3.5 while using two of eight bands, and notebook 05 §3 had leaned on `pred_range` as its compression warning), so the band marginal with a `bands used` row is now printed beside the metrics; and **spread is not calibration** (the untrained expectation decode has predicted sd 0.594 against the fine-tuned 0.391 and human 0.733 — better-spread and three times worse at ranking). Notebook 05 re-executed with every pre-existing figure reproducing exactly (RF 0.558, the WER mediation table, the gold ablation), so the new §4 is an addition rather than a disturbance; licence scan re-run clean. Report Recommendation 1 now requires an arm to report its own untrained or ablated floor, not only to beat the word-count baseline. Stated as weak evidence rather than a finding: +0.302 from 400 iterations at rank 8 is a steep part of a curve, which slightly raises the prior that scale is worth testing. Full detail: `docs/session-2026-08-28.md`.

## 2026-08-27 — LoRA transcript scorer: the content-aware arm loses (notebook 05)

Built and ran a fifth scoring arm in one day — a 1.5B instruct model LoRA-fine-tuned on transcripts and decoded ordinally — to answer Recommendation 3 of the evaluation report: add content features, then re-run the ASR-propagation analysis, since the published null was conditional on content-blind features. **The headline is a negative result, and it is the finding: QWK 0.446 (argmax) / 0.415 (expected) against Random Forest's 0.558** on identical rows under an identical protocol, finishing *below* the word-count Ridge floor of 0.500 that the notebook nominated in advance as its honesty bar — last of the four real arms. Pooled r = 0.55 is real signal, so the arm ranks candidates; it just ranks them less well than fluency timing does at 1.5B and rank 8. **Recommendation 3 is answered against the caveat it was raised to test:** a scorer reading nothing but the transcript reproduces the same mediation (raw r = +0.161 → partial −0.036), and the RF row reproduces the published +0.15 → −0.01 to three decimals, validating the whole path. Two design assumptions died on contact with data. First, expectation decoding was supposed to be *the* result and isn't — argmax beats it on QWK (0.446 vs 0.415) while losing on correlation (0.505 vs 0.550), because the decodes trade ranking accuracy against scale fidelity and QWK, computed on the snapped 0.5 grid, charges for compression where Pearson r does not; both are now reported, neither is "the" answer. Second, the gold-transcript ablation was described as changing the input and holding everything else fixed — it does not (589 rows vs 876 means 425 training rows per fold against 630 and ~3.8 epochs against ~2.5, all pushing the same way), so the causal claim was withdrawn rather than softened, and the clean control is named as outstanding work. Content made compression *worse*, not better: +1.028 / −1.012 at the band edges against RF's +0.96 / −0.70, exact agreement 27.6% against 34.0%. Protocol findings worth as much as the headline: one fold in five collapsed to two output classes (`[0,0,63,0,113,0,0,0]` — nothing on 3.5, one of the two commonest human scores), and three of five folds ended on weights measurably worse than ones the run passed through, since `save_every` was set to `iters` so no best-checkpoint existed to fall back on — which makes the pooled 0.446 a mild under-estimate. The temperature sweep measures the compression account rather than asserting it (QWK rises monotonically as the distribution sharpens while r stays flat at ~0.55 — temperature moves scale, not ranking) and recovers only ~+0.03 before running out; T stayed at 1.0, because selecting 0.25 off that table would be tuning on the evaluation set. The build half: `speechlab.ordinal` and `speechlab.llm_scorer` plus `scripts/finetune_lora.py`, running `mlx_lm lora` as a **subprocess per fold** after an audit of the installed MLX source turned up six per-process footguns that a subprocess boundary sidesteps structurally; full suite **51 passed**. Gold-transcript coverage (67–68.5% of dev P3+P4) rather than compute was the binding constraint, so the plan became dev-audio Whisper transcription — 3.5 hours — to keep all 876 rows comparable to the published baseline. Several silent failures were closed before they could bite at the end of a six-hour pipeline: prompt parity verified against the real Qwen2.5 tokenizer rather than the test stub, prompt lengths measured against `max_seq_length` (median 168, max 269 vs 1024 — mlx-lm truncates without complaint), a missing `extract_prosody.py` step that the comparison section needed, and a default kernelspec pointing at an Anaconda python lacking `speechlab` and `mlx` but *having* `sklearn`, which would have made a kernel failure look like a notebook bug. Notebook 05 is committed with outputs per the 01–04 convention, checked cell by cell: 0 utterance ids, 0 speaker ids and 0 verbatim five-word transcript runs across its full text output, re-measured against the corpus; adapters stay local, since a model trained on learner transcripts is a licence-bound derivative. `reports/` updated to match. Full detail: `docs/session-2026-08-27.md`.

## 2026-06-05 — ASR error analysis and prosody (notebook 02)

Measured what notebook 01 could only infer: whisper-small scores **16.8% WER** against gold transcripts (65/100 sample responses are gold-covered), rising as proficiency falls (r = −0.28) — ASR noise concentrates on the weakest speakers, now a core fairness-analysis thread. Gold annotations confirm **100% filled-pause erasure** (333 spoken → 0 transcribed) and show the erased signal is predictive (r = −0.25). Swapping TTR for MTLD lifted QWK to **0.647** (best so far); five Praat prosody features added nothing at n = 100 — a reported null result, with `intensity_sd_db` flagged as a likely recording-gain confound. Also caught a CV reproducibility trap: row order silently changes fold assignment (±0.04 QWK at this n). Afternoon: extracted the notebook helpers into a src-layout `speechlab` package with a 23-test pytest suite on synthetic fixtures only (licence rule) and an equivalence check reproducing notebook 02's QWK to the third decimal; the tests caught a real MTLD boundary bug. Full-dev transcription (5,616 responses) started via a resumable script. Evening: notebooks 03–04 at full scale (876 P3+P4 responses, speaker-grouped CV). Headline corrected: **RF QWK 0.558** pooled — the n=100 figure (0.647) was small-sample optimism, and the public README now carries the honest number. Speaker leakage measured ≈0; PyTorch MLP an honest null. Error analysis: band 2 over-scored +0.96 / band 5 under-scored −0.70 (scale compression), 81.5% agreement within half a band, and the nb02 ASR-propagation alarm resolved as proficiency mediation (partial r = −0.01). Audio-quality fairness slice flat but thin; per-speaker error tail documented. Closed the core roadmap with the written deliverables: `reports/model_card.md` and `reports/evaluation_report.md` — agreement, error analysis, ASR impact and fairness assembled from the four notebooks, word-count baseline framed as the bar any improvement must clear. In one day the project went from a 100-response vertical slice to a complete, published evaluation lab. Full detail: `docs/session-2026-06-05.md`.
